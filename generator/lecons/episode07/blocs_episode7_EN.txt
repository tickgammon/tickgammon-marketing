The real value of the computer is that it shows you your mistakes. After a game, you can have it analysed move by move.

For each move, the analysis shows you the best possible choice, and compares it with the one you played.

When you got it wrong, it tells you how much that mistake cost you. A small inaccuracy isn't a big deal. A big mistake, on the other hand, is one you need to understand.

Don't try to fix everything at once. Focus on the two or three biggest mistakes of the game. That's where you'll improve fastest.

An important tip: don't just look at the result of the game.

A win can hide big mistakes, because the dice saved you. And a loss can come from the dice, despite perfect play.

The result partly depends on luck. The analysis only looks at your decisions. It's the one that tells the truth! [laughs]

On TickGammon, the Training module goes even further. It has you work on selected positions, and it analyses your moves.

You play a position, then you see straight away whether your move was right, and what the best one was.

It's the fastest way to fix your habits: openings, hitting, the race, the doubling cube… everything you've seen in these lessons.

And with the Wisdom Pack, the artificial intelligence can even explain your mistake in plain language, and the analysis goes deeper.

Instead of just a number, you understand why one move is better than another. And that's what really makes you improve.

Here's a simple method for improving against the computer, in three steps.

Step one: play a full game, calmly, without looking at any hints during play.

Step two: have the game analysed, and write down your two or three biggest mistakes.

Step three: replay those positions in Training until you find the right move first time. Then start again with a new game.

Are you a beginner, worried that it's too hard? It's normal to lose often at first.

But every game against a strong opponent teaches you something. And little by little, your mistakes become rarer and smaller.

If needed, go back over the previous lessons: the rules, the openings, the doubling cube, the race and the probabilities. It all comes together on the board against the bot.

And when you feel ready, move on to online games against real players!

And there you go, you now know how to use the computer to improve: an opponent that's always available, honest dice, and an analysis that shows you your mistakes.
